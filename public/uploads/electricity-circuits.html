Electricity & Circuits

The Big Idea

Electricity is just electrons moving through a wire. When they move, they carry energy. If we control that movement with a circuit, we can light a bulb, run a phone, or cook nsima. Every circuit is a loop — the current must have a path out and a path back.

Simple Explanation

Think of a bicycle chain. The pedals push the chain, the chain turns the wheel. If the chain breaks, nothing moves.

An electric circuit works the same way: - The battery = your legs (pushes the electrons) - The wire = the chain (carries them) - The bulb/motor = the wheel (does the useful work) - The switch = a brake that stops the chain

If any part breaks, the whole loop stops. That is why a broken bulb in a series circuit switches off the whole string.

The three key quantities

QuantitySymbolUnitMeaning
CurrentIAmpere (A)How much charge flows per second
VoltageVVolt (V)The “push” given to the electrons
ResistanceROhm (Ω)How much the component slows the flow

Ohm’s Law

V = I × R

Rearranged: - I = V/R - R = V/I

Series vs Parallel

Series: one path. Current is the same everywhere. Voltages add. Rtotal = R1 + R2 + R3

Parallel: many paths. Voltage is the same across each branch. Currents add. $$\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$$

Electrical Power

P = V × I

Also useful: - P = I2R - P = V2/R

Unit of power: Watt (W). Energy used: E = P × t (in Joules, or kWh for bills).

Key Points Box

  • Current is the same everywhere in a series circuit.
  • Voltage is the same across every branch in a parallel circuit.
  • Ohm’s Law: V = IR. Learn all three rearrangements.
  • Series resistors add up.
  • Parallel resistors always give a smaller total than the smallest one.
  • Power: P = VI. Energy: E = Pt.
  • Ammeters go in series with a component.
  • Voltmeters go in parallel across a component.

Worked Example

Question: A 12 V battery is connected to two resistors in series: R1 = 4 Ω and R2 = 8 Ω. Find (a) total resistance, (b) current, (c) voltage across each resistor, (d) power used by R2.

Solution:

(a) Total resistance (series): Rtotal = R1 + R2 = 4 + 8 = 12 Ω

(b) Current (Ohm’s Law): $$I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{12}{12} = 1\,\text{A}$$

(c) Voltage across each resistor: - V1 = I × R1 = 1 × 4 = 4 V - V2 = I × R2 = 1 × 8 = 8 V - Check: 4 + 8 = 12 V ✅ (matches the battery)

(d) Power in R2: P = V2 × I = 8 × 1 = 8 W

Exam-Style Questions (MSCE)

Q1. A student connects three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 5 Ω in series to a 20 V supply. Calculate: (a) the total resistance, (b) the current in the circuit, (c) the voltage across the 5 Ω resistor, (d) the power dissipated in the 3 Ω resistor.

Q2. Two resistors, R1 = 6 Ω and R2 = 3 Ω, are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery. (a) Find the total resistance. (b) Find the total current from the battery. (c) Find the current through each resistor. (d) Show that the currents add to your answer in (b).

Q3. Explain why the total resistance of resistors in parallel is always less than the smallest individual resistor.

Q4. An electric iron is rated 240 V, 1000 W. (a) Calculate the current it draws. (b) Calculate its resistance. (c) If used for 30 minutes, how much energy (in kWh) does it use?

Q5. A 12 V car battery is connected to a bulb of resistance 6 Ω and an ammeter. Draw the circuit, then calculate the current. Where must the ammeter be placed, and why?

Answers

Check Yourself: 1. Ohm (Ω) 2. The same 3. R = V/I = 6/2 = 3 Ω 4. 1/R = 1/10 + 1/10 = 2/10 → R = 5 Ω 5. V = IR, I = V/R, R = V/I

Q1. (a) R = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 Ω (b) I = 20/10 = 2 A (c) V = 2 × 5 = 10 V (d) P = I2R = 22 × 3 = 12 W

Q2. (a) 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 → R = 2 Ω (b) I = 12/2 = 6 A (c) I1 = 12/6 = 2 A; I2 = 12/3 = 4 A (d) 2 + 4 = 6 A ✅

Q3. In parallel, each resistor gives the current another path. More paths = easier flow = less overall resistance. The total can never be smaller than the smallest path alone; adding more paths only makes it easier still.

Q4. (a) I = P/V = 1000/240 ≈ 4.17 A (b) R = V/I = 240/4.17 ≈ 57.6 Ω (or R = V2/P = 57600/1000 = 57.6 Ω) (c) E = P × t = 1 kW × 0.5 h = 0.5 kWh

Q5. Circuit: battery → ammeter → bulb → back to battery (series loop). I = V/R = 12/6 = 2 A. The ammeter must be in series because it measures the current through a component — all the current must pass through it.

Exam Tip

Examiners love asking students to compare series and parallel in the same question. Always write down what stays the same: - Series → current is the same, voltages add - Parallel → voltage is the same, currents add

Also: if your final answer has the wrong unit, you lose a mark even if the number is right. Always write the unit.

Remember

A circuit is a loop. No loop, no current. Ohm’s Law is your friend: V = IR — and the two rearrangements must be automatic.
